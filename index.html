How to compose it

Sony α7 II · Camera setup

Before you shoot

  1. Turn the top mode dial to M (Manual Exposure).
  2. MENU → Camera Settings → Quality → RAW or RAW & JPEG.
  3. Set the recommended aperture with the front dial and shutter speed with the rear dial (unless you changed dial assignments).
  4. Choose ISO from the camera controls; start at the listed value, then adjust while checking the meter and histogram.
  5. MENU → White Balance → Color Temp. and enter the suggested Kelvin value.

Focus and exposure

Still subjects: AF-S + Flexible Spot (S or M). Moving subjects: AF-C + Zone or Lock-on AF. Portraits: turn on Face Detection when helpful, but check that the nearest eye is sharp. This model does not have modern real-time Eye AF tracking.

Handheld: SteadyShot ON. Long exposures on a stable tripod: try SteadyShot OFF. Use the self-timer to avoid shaking the camera.

Limits: shutter 1/8000–30 seconds (Bulb for longer); standard ISO 100–25,600, with ISO 50 extension. Your kit lens opens to f/3.5 at 28mm and f/5.6 at 70mm.

Use the histogram to verify exposure. Bright backlit faces and very dark backgrounds often require changes from the starting values.

3 · Make it work in the field

Five-second check

  1. Set RAW (or RAW+JPEG), if available.
  2. Match aperture, shutter, ISO and white balance.
  3. Focus on the intended subject.
  4. Take a test frame and check histogram/highlight warnings.
  5. Adjust exposure and repeat as light changes.
No settings guarantee an identical result every time: clouds, light direction, subject movement, focal length, stabilization and sensor performance change exposure. These are field-tested-style starting ranges, not a camera light-meter replacement.

Favorites & field notes

Exposure cheat sheet

Aperture (f-number)

Smaller f-number = more light and shallower background blur. Larger f-number = more depth of field, but less light.

Shutter speed

Faster = freezes motion but needs more light. Slower = blur / camera shake risk; tripod helps.

ISO

Raise it when shutter and aperture must stay fixed; higher ISO can increase noise.

White balance

Kelvin sets colour, not brightness. RAW lets you change it later more easily.
